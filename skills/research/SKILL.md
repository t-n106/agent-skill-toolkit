# Research

Apply within the user/host task boundary; this skill adds no authority. Use the current task context when no separate orchestration layer exists.

## Evidence acquisition
1. Define the question, scope, required date/version, and evidence that could affect the answer. Avoid expensive investigation of unresolved product choices before they matter.
2. Choose sources appropriate to the claim: original documentation, filings, datasets, standards, or research for primary facts; reliable secondary sources for discovery and interpretation; community accounts for experience or practice. Relevance matters more than prestige.
3. Check date, version, period, and applicability for changeable facts. Historical evidence is not automatically current evidence.
4. For material uncertainty, seek contradiction or scope limitations as well as support. One decisive primary source can outweigh many derivative summaries; source count is not independence.
5. Resolve disagreements by checking definitions, periods, methods, scope, and corrections. Preserve genuine unresolved conflicts rather than averaging them away.
6. Retain provenance for material claims: source identifier/link and relevant section, date, or dataset version. Separate source statements, derived inference, and hypotheses; preserve qualifiers.

## Stop and deliver
Stop when the question is sufficiently answered, further evidence is unlikely to change the result, or useful evidence is unavailable/disproportionately costly. Preserve material gaps and their consequences; do not claim completeness merely because searching stopped.

Return supported facts, qualified/disputed findings, and relevant gaps with provenance. A routine answer does not require a separate synthesis skill; use `analysis` or `synthesis-decision` only for a substantive interpretation or trade-off problem.
